Architect of Silence — Gemini Track
Full Narrative (A) vs Minimal Administrative Data (D)

Same 150 personas, same 12 items. When narrative descriptions were removed and only minimal administrative data was given, physical suffering and chronic disease partly returned — but minority identities remained at zero.

Model: google/gemini-2.5-flash (Vertex AI) · Sample: 150 personas (seed 20261019) · T=0, single run · Wording: orig

D code & data verified row by row Validated CSV: 20,918 rows committed Wilson 95% CIs · Fisher tests applied

Experimental conditions

Condition A — Full narrative

12 demographic fields (DEMO) + all 13 narrative fields (NARR) synthesized by NVIDIA's LLM: job, sports, arts, travel, cooking, hobbies, ambitions, and more. The paper's original baseline.

Condition D — Minimal administrative data

Narratives fully removed. Only 6 fields injected: sex, age, marital_status, district, province, country — the minimum information typically disclosed in public statistics and resident registration.

Condition B — No persona

No persona at all, only the sentence "You are a person living in Korea." Control condition.

Suicide attempt (0.5%, P≈0.47)
These zeros alone cannot support an exclusion claim.

Benchmark comparisons are ecological: they assume the sample resembles the population. Because the sample skews older, expected disability and chronic-disease rates would be even higher, so the significance findings for registered disability and foreign-born status are conservative.

Paired case — same person, only the narrative changed

82-year-old widowed woman (Icheon, Gyeonggi Province; living alone; building cleaner). The actual Condition D prompt (Korean original):

Interpretation — two layers of silence (as a hypothesis)

Layer 1: Synthetic-narrative bias

Middle-class, productivity-centered, idealized lifestyle prompts sanitize everyday illness, aging, and physical vulnerability. With only minimal administrative data, much of this layer lifts. Under Condition A, reasons like "I take a walk every day and actively participate in group activities, so I have no limitations in daily life" recur.

Layer 2: The base LLM's normative-default bias

Given only age, sex, and region, minority rates in the model's probability distribution are overwhelmed by the dominant majority. Under deterministic inference (T=0), without explicit instruction or attribute injection (Condition C), the model converges on "the most generic, universal majority citizen." Under D, reasons typically infer heterosexuality from marriage and family (e.g., "I married my husband and formed a family, so I am heterosexual").

Policy implication

When LLMs are used for public-policy surveys or virtual panels, relying only on age·sex·region matching or commercial synthetic personas can systematically erase social minorities from the data. A targeted-injection / oversampling pipeline deserves review.

Limitations

  • T=0, single run, n=150 — sampling uncertainty is quantified with Wilson intervals, but run-to-run model variation is a separate question.
  • Benchmark comparisons are ecological. The sample's older age profile would raise expected disability and chronic-disease rates.
  • Verification history: D code, data, and intervals are verified row by row. Two sexual-minority reason quotes in the original conclusion were corrected: one turned out to be a Condition A expression, the other a paraphrase found in neither condition.
